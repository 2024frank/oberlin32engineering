# Website content standard

Write for an Oberlin student who is scanning for one answer: what is available, what is confirmed, and what they should do next.

## Page structure

- State the page purpose in the `h1` and first paragraph.
- Put the current status before background or aspiration.
- Give each page one job. Remove sections that restate the introduction in different words.
- Use headings that name a task or subject. Avoid slogans that need another sentence to explain them.
- Give links and buttons labels that describe the destination or action.
- Keep one idea per paragraph. Prefer sentences under 25 words when the meaning survives the edit.

## Voice

- Use plain, direct language and active verbs.
- Prefer names, dates, owners, constraints, and observable results over adjectives.
- Distinguish current facts, conditional plans, and future ideas.
- Say when funding, dates, rooms, people, or approvals are not confirmed.
- Do not promise admission outcomes, funding, partnerships, attendance, or impact.

## Patterns to remove

These patterns are not proof that AI wrote a passage. They are editing warnings because they often hide weak information:

- “It is not just X; it is Y” and similar staged contrasts.
- Repeated lists of exactly 3 abstract ideas.
- “Whether you are…” introductions that try to address every possible reader.
- Generic claims about community, impact, innovation, journeys, or transformation without a named action or result.
- Rephrasing the same point in the introduction, section heading, and conclusion.
- Neat, optimistic conclusions that add no next step.
- Detailed frameworks for programs, events, or review processes that do not exist yet.
- Symmetrical three-card or four-step structures used to make a thin idea look complete.
- Repeated use of “founding,” “current,” “official,” or “confirmed” where one clear status statement is enough.
- Em dashes in public copy. Use a period, comma, colon, or a new sentence.
- “Not as a substitute,” “not just,” and similar negative disclaimers. State the required action directly.
- “Serves as,” “meant to,” and “a place to.” Name the person, action, or service instead.

Do not make text deliberately rough to appear human. Good copy can be polished. It should also be specific, varied, and accountable to evidence.

## Founding-stage pages

State what exists before describing what may happen. If no project, event, report, sponsor, or partnership exists, say so directly. Do not fill an empty page with invented formats or an imagined workflow. Keep the route only when it gives the visitor a useful status or action.

## Dynamic content

Static headings must remain true when database records are empty or populated. Use neutral headings such as “Current project briefs” or “Event plans.” Let the data component own the empty-state message.
