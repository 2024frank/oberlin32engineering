About the society

A home for engineering
at Oberlin.

We are building a durable student community for people who want to understand engineering, pursue the 3-2 pathway, create useful work, and help one another move forward.

CONNECTPREPAREBUILD
The reason we exist

The problem is not a lack of talent.

It is fragmentation.

Engineering-minded students at Oberlin can sit in the same physics lecture, work in adjacent labs, or dream about similar futures without ever realizing they should know one another.

Current and prospective 3-2 students move through several departments. Older students leave for engineering schools just as their experience becomes most valuable. Project ideas appear, but teams, materials, continuity, and visibility can be difficult to assemble.

The Oberlin 3-2 Engineering Society is designed to connect those pieces. It gives students a shared identity, a reliable place to find guidance, a structure for building, and a way to pass knowledge forward.

Our operating question
What can we create together that none of us could build alone?
MISSION

Connect students. Clarify the path. Build real things.

Bring current and prospective 3-2 students together, connect them with resources and people, and create opportunities to practice engineering through interdisciplinary work.

VISION

A lasting engineering culture within Oberlin's liberal arts community.

Future students should arrive to an existing network, inherit useful knowledge, find collaborators quickly, and see engineering as part of Oberlin rather than something that begins only after leaving.

How we will work

Principles before programming.

A good club is not measured by how many ideas it announces. It is measured by whether people trust it, learn through it, and finish meaningful work.

01

Open by design

Membership is open to every Oberlin student interested in engineering, design, technology, innovation, or building.

02

Useful outcomes

Events and projects should create a clear result, not simply fill a calendar.

03

Document the work

Plans, budgets, failures, resources, and lessons should remain useful after current leaders leave.

04

Build responsibly

Safety, consent, accessibility, ethical judgment, and respect for community partners are part of engineering quality.

05

Finish before expanding

A few completed projects and well-run programs are better than a long list of abandoned promises.

06

Make belonging practical

People stay when they are welcomed, trusted with real responsibility, and able to see how they contribute.

Designed to last

The society cannot depend on one person.

Leadership transition is part of the system, not an emergency at the end of the year.

01

Shared records

Meeting notes, contacts, budgets, project files, and decisions live in organization-owned spaces.

02

Defined handoffs

Every officer leaves a role guide, current priorities, recurring tasks, and a clear next action.

03

Student voice

Members help choose projects, programming priorities, and improvements through structured feedback.

04

Truthful communication

Plans, approvals, funding, and outcomes are described accurately. Proposed work is never presented as confirmed.
