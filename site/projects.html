Project philosophy

The goal is not to look technical.

The goal is to understand a problem, make responsible choices, integrate different skills, test honestly, and communicate evidence.

Projects should be small enough to finish, meaningful enough to matter, and documented well enough for another student to reproduce, critique, or continue.

The build cycle

From question to demonstration.

A visible process helps teams avoid rushing into parts before they understand the problem.

Join a project team
01

Frame

Who is affected? What is the actual problem? What would meaningful improvement look like?

02

Scope

Choose a version that can be completed with the available time, skills, materials, and safety support.

03

Prototype

Build the smallest version that can answer the most important uncertainty.

04

Test

Collect evidence, identify failure modes, revise assumptions, and improve the design.

05

Document

Record the design, code, bill of materials, decisions, limitations, and next steps.

06

Share

Demonstrate the result, explain what failed, and leave the next team a stronger starting point.

Before a project begins

Good engineering includes the conditions around the build.

Funding is never the only requirement. Teams must also confirm supervision, access, storage, safety, consent, and responsible use.

Safety and training

Tools, batteries, electronics, chemicals, fabrication, fieldwork, and moving systems require appropriate procedures and oversight.

Community and user consent

Projects involving people or community partners are developed with them, not merely placed on them.

Clear success criteria

Teams agree on the evidence that would show progress, failure, or the need to change direction.

Open documentation

Project files should remain accessible to the society unless privacy, security, or partner agreements require limits.
