Oberlin 3-2 guide

Plan the five-year path with better questions.

Use this page to prepare for advising conversations, compare partner schools, and track decisions. Treat official Oberlin and partner-school guidance as the final authority.

Open Oberlin’s official guide ↗See the planning timeline
At a glance

Students ordinarily spend three years at Oberlin and two years at an affiliated engineering school, completing degrees from both institutions.

Check financial aid early.
The engineering institution handles aid during the engineering portion, and the package may differ from Oberlin’s.

Planning timeline

Do not leave the important questions for year three.

Your exact course plan depends on preparation, intended engineering field, partner school, and Oberlin degree requirements.

Before or during year 1

Start the math and science sequence

Discuss placement, calculus, physics, chemistry, computer science, and possible engineering fields with an adviser.

Ask: Which courses must be taken in sequence, and what happens if one is delayed?

Year 1 to year 2

Compare partner requirements

Requirements are not identical. Build a table of prerequisites, grade expectations, application dates, financial-aid processes, and degree options.

Ask: Which requirements apply to my intended major and entry year?

Year 2

Check the whole Oberlin degree

Review major, distribution, writing, residence, and other graduation requirements instead of looking only at engineering prerequisites.

Ask: What must be completed before leaving Oberlin, and what may be completed through transfer credit?

Year 2 to year 3

Plan the application and transition

Confirm application materials, recommendations, transcripts, housing, transportation, visa implications where relevant, and financial aid.

Ask: What deadlines are controlled by Oberlin, and which are controlled by the partner school?

Year 3

Verify everything in writing

Before leaving Oberlin, confirm the course record, degree plan, application status, aid package, and expected path to both degrees.

Ask: Who should I contact when a requirement or transfer decision is unclear?

Affiliated institutions

Four partner schools, different programs.

Open each official page and verify the requirements for your expected application year. Policies and deadlines can change.

Do not assume every partner still follows the same two-year format. WashU currently describes its Dual Degree Program as three additional years leading to an engineering bachelor’s and master’s. Verify the current structure for your entry year.

Advising meeting checklist

Bring specific questions.

  • My intended Oberlin major and possible engineering field
  • My current math and science placement
  • A draft five-year course plan
  • Partner prerequisites I have checked
  • Questions about financial aid and total cost
  • Questions about housing, credit transfer, and degree timing
  • Backup options if I change fields or stay four years at Oberlin

Keep a decision log

After every advising meeting, record the date, person you met, questions asked, answers received, official links, and anything that still needs confirmation.

!
Peer advice is context, not policy.

A useful student story can help you know what to ask. It cannot replace a current written requirement.

Still exploring?

You can join before deciding on 3-2.

Many students need time to understand the academic path, costs, engineering fields, and alternatives. The society is a place to ask those questions without pretending the decision is already made.
