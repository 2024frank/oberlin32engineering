Aim

Bring Oberlin’s engineering community into one room.

The Oberlin 3-2 Engineering Society exists so that students at Oberlin who are interested in engineering can find each other, build things together, and get clear answers about the 3-2 pathway.

Engineering interest at Oberlin is spread across physics, computer science, mathematics, chemistry, environmental studies, TIMARA, and other departments. That range is a strength, but it means students often work alone and learn what they need by accident. The society brings them into one place.

To do that, we aim to:

  1. 01

    Build a community across departments and class years, open to anyone interested in engineering whether or not they intend to transfer.

  2. 02

    Run hands-on projects a student team can scope, finish, and document within a semester.

  3. 03

    Make the 3-2 pathway easier to navigate by collecting the questions students actually have and pointing to current official advising.

  4. 04

    Hold regular meetups, workshops, and conversations with engineers and alumni.

What we do

Projects, pathway support, and technical programs.

The society’s work is organized around three areas. Activities are published only after the people, place, schedule, and resources are confirmed.

Engineering projects

Members select practical problems, form teams, define a realistic scope, build and test prototypes, and document the results.

3-2 pathway support

Students compare questions, partner-program information, and planning timelines using current official sources and Oberlin advising.

Technical programs

The society organizes workshops, project sessions, and conversations with faculty, alumni, engineers, and researchers.

The academic pathway

Plan the transfer before the application year.

Course sequences, admission rules, financial aid, housing, and degree timelines differ by partner school. The club helps students compare questions and prepare for official advising.

The idea behind the club

Engineering should be something Oberlin students can do together.

Kwaku started the society after seeing the same problem from different sides: students interested in engineering were scattered across campus, students considering 3-2 had questions that were hard to compare, and people who wanted to build had no obvious team to join.

The idea is to give that interest a home. A physics student, a TIMARA student, a programmer, a designer, and someone who has never touched a breadboard should be able to meet here, find a useful role, and make something together.

Frank (Kwaku) Kusi AppiahFounder and president
